# Mud Island (Memphis, TN) and Wolf River Erosion/Water Damage Research (Last 5 Years)

Date compiled: 2026-03-19  
Scope window: 2021-03-19 through 2026-03-19  
Focus: Erosion or water damage along the Wolf River side of Mud Island/nearby reaches that could contribute to foundation risk or backyard/bank edge loss.

## Executive Findings

- **Direct evidence of dangerous bank erosion exists in the Wolf River corridor (2024-2025).**  
  A U.S. Army Corps of Engineers Memphis District release (Dec 2024) states erosion near MLGW Tower #1613 had advanced to about **99 feet from the tower foundation**, creating imminent collapse risk into the Wolf River.  
  Source: [USACE Memphis news release (Dec 2024)](https://www.mvm.usace.army.mil/Media/News-Releases/Article/4000938/usace-memphis-releases-draft-feasibility-report-environmental-assessment-for-ml/)

- **Mud Island-adjacent flooding and sediment/erosion impacts were reported during the April 2025 event.**  
  Wolf River Conservancy reported historic flooding with Greenway damage from erosion, plus the lower North Mud Island section underwater.  
  Source: [Wolf River Conservancy, Apr 22 2025](https://www.wolfriver.org/news-press/flooding-impacts-the-wolf-river-greenway)

- **Hydrologic conditions in April 2025 were severe enough to materially increase erosion potential.**  
  Local Memphis weather reporting and event recaps describe Wolf River flood-stage conditions and very high multi-day rainfall totals.  
  Sources:  
  - [localmemphis / ABC24: Wolf River hits flood stage](https://www.localmemphis.com/article/weather/weather-impact/memphis-wolf-river-flood-stage-moderate-severe-storms-rain/522-d8aef603-e503-47b4-a715-7e2fd6adc122)  
  - [MemphisWeather recap, Apr 2025](https://memphisweather.net/blog/2025/04/12/recap-of-severe-weather-and-flooding-event-april-2-5-2025)

- **Publicly available sources in this 5-year window do not clearly document a specific Mud Island residence foundation failure caused by Wolf River bank erosion.**  
  However, multiple sources indicate conditions that can precede those outcomes (bank scour, prolonged high water, saturated soils, and flood loading).

## Evidence Log (2021-2026)

### 1) USACE emergency streambank/shoreline erosion study (Dec 2024)
- **What it says:** USACE Memphis announced a draft feasibility report and EA for emergency streambank and shoreline erosion at the Wolf River near MLGW Tower #1613; erosion reportedly within ~99 feet of foundation.  
- **Why it matters for homes:** This is direct governmental evidence that Wolf River bank migration/scour can threaten structural foundations in this watershed.  
- **Strength:** High (federal agency release).  
- Source: [USACE release](https://www.mvm.usace.army.mil/Media/News-Releases/Article/4000938/usace-memphis-releases-draft-feasibility-report-environmental-assessment-for-ml/)

### 2) Wolf River Conservancy flood/erosion impact statement (Apr 2025)
- **What it says:** April 2025 flooding caused significant/dangerous riverbank erosion damage on Greenway sections; Mud Island north section was underwater; FEMA/USACE engagement noted to reestablish eroded banks.  
- **Why it matters for homes/backyards:** The same erosive processes affecting nearby linear infrastructure can affect private bank edges and potentially reduce setback distances over time.  
- **Strength:** Medium-high (regional conservation organization coordinating with public agencies).  
- Source: [Flooding Impacts the Wolf River Greenway](https://www.wolfriver.org/news-press/flooding-impacts-the-wolf-river-greenway)

### 3) April 2025 hydrologic severity indicators (media + weather recap)
- **What it says:** Wolf River reached flood stage (near major at one gauge in reporting), and regional rainfall was extreme over multiple days.  
- **Why it matters for homes/backyards:** Rapid stage rises, repeated inundation, and saturated banks increase sloughing, toe erosion, and lateral bank retreat risk.  
- **Strength:** Medium (journalistic + meteorological recap rather than engineering post-event reports).  
- Sources:  
  - [localmemphis flood-stage report](https://www.localmemphis.com/article/weather/weather-impact/memphis-wolf-river-flood-stage-moderate-severe-storms-rain/522-d8aef603-e503-47b4-a715-7e2fd6adc122)  
  - [MemphisWeather severe weather/flood recap](https://memphisweather.net/blog/2025/04/12/recap-of-severe-weather-and-flooding-event-april-2-5-2025)

### 4) FEMA/TEMA declaration context (Apr 2025)
- **What it indicates:** Tennessee received emergency federal support after severe storms/flooding during the same event window.  
- **Why it matters:** Confirms broad-scale event severity and potential for public/private damage pathways, including bank instability and water intrusion issues.  
- **Strength:** Medium (event-level context; not parcel-level damage confirmation).  
- References discovered during research:  
  - [FEMA emergency declaration press release URL](https://www.fema.gov/press-release/20250403/president-donald-j-trump-approves-emergency-declaration-tennessee)

## How This Translates to Foundation or Backyard Erosion Risk

Potential mechanisms relevant to Mud Island/Wolf River-side properties:

- **Bank toe scour during high flow:** Removes support at the base of slope, increasing slumping risk.
- **Drawdown + saturation cycles:** Rapid water-level change can destabilize fine-grained banks.
- **Overbank flooding and prolonged wetness:** Increases hydrostatic pressure, seepage, and softening around shallow foundations or retaining edges.
- **Sediment loss and channel migration:** Can reduce horizontal distance between structures/fences and bank edge over time.

## What Is Confirmed vs. Not Yet Confirmed

### Confirmed in the 2021-2026 window
- Significant flood/erosion impacts occurred in 2025 affecting Wolf River corridors including Mud Island-adjacent segments.
- A Wolf River erosion case in Memphis was severe enough in 2024 to trigger federal emergency engineering study due to foundation-threat proximity.

### Not clearly confirmed in public sources reviewed
- A documented, publicly reported **Mud Island residential foundation failure** directly attributed to Wolf River bank erosion during this 5-year period.
- A publicly available parcel-by-parcel inventory of backyard erosion losses specifically for Harbor Town/Mud Island homes.
